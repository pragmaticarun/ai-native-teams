## Costs to include

- **Licences and usage** (seats, API tokens, platform fees). Agentic use can make usage costs grow quickly, so track them ([M8](/ai-native-teams/m08-evals/observability/#cost)).
- **People time:** building, maintaining, evaluating, training, champions.
- **Governance:** reviews, security, legal.
- **Change costs:** temporary productivity dips while people learn.

## Benefits to include

- **Time reinvested**, valued by what it's reinvested *in* (more customer work, faster delivery, reduced backlog), not just hours "saved".
- **Quality gains:** fewer errors, less rework, fewer incidents.
- **Speed to outcome:** faster cycle times that matter commercially (time to market, time to resolution).
- **New capabilities:** things that weren't feasible before (24/7 first-line support, personalised onboarding, prototypes in days).
- **Risk reduction:** consistent processes, audit trails.

## A simple ROI frame

```text
Annual benefit  = Σ over workflows (measured improvement × volume × value per unit)
Annual cost     = licences + usage + people time + governance
ROI             = (benefit − cost) / cost
Payback period  = upfront cost / monthly net benefit
```

Show **ranges** (conservative / expected), state your assumptions, and separate **measured** from **estimated** figures. DORA has published an ROI framework and calculator specifically for AI-assisted software development that is a useful reference for engineering teams ([DORA ROI of AI-assisted Software Development](https://dora.dev/ai/roi/report/)).

## Telling the value story

A good quarterly AI value update fits on one page:

1. **What we changed** (workflows redesigned, tools adopted).
2. **What moved** (outcome, quality, experience metrics vs baseline, with ranges).
3. **What didn't work** and what we learned.
4. **Costs** and how they're trending.
5. **What's next** and what we need (budget, decisions, help).

Including what didn't work increases credibility. Sponsors have seen too many AI success stories without evidence.
