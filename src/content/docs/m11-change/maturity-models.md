Maturity models give a shared language for "where are we, and what's next?". They differ a lot in scope and evidence, so use them as maps, not scorecards.

## Organisation-wide models

| Model | Structure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **MIT CISR Enterprise AI Maturity Model** (Dec 2024) | 4 stages: Experiment & Prepare → Build Pilots & Capabilities → Develop AI Ways of Working → Become AI Future Ready. Based on a survey of 721 companies; firms in stages 3–4 had above-average financial performance | [MIT CISR](https://cisr.mit.edu/publication/2024_1201_EnterpriseAIMaturityModel_WeillWoernerSebastian) |
| **Gartner AI Maturity Model** | 5 stages; Gartner's current assessment page uses Foundational → Emerging → Operational → Scaled → Transformational, across seven pillars | [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/chief-information-officer/research/ai-maturity-model-toolkit) |
| **Google Cloud AI Adoption Framework** (2020) | Six themes (Learn, Lead, Access, Scale, Automate, Secure), each assessed as Tactical → Strategic → Transformational | [Whitepaper](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/ai_adoption_framework_whitepaper.pdf) |
| **BCG "Widening AI Value Gap"** (Sep 2025) | Segments: future-built (5%), scaling (35%), and laggards (60%) | [BCG](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/are-you-generating-value-from-ai-the-widening-gap) |

## Team and engineering-level models

| Model | Structure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **DORA AI Capabilities Model** (2025) | Not levels: seven capabilities that amplify AI's benefits (clear AI stance, healthy data ecosystems, AI-accessible internal data, strong version control, small batches, user-centric focus, quality internal platforms) | [DORA](https://dora.dev/ai/capabilities-model/report/) |
| **Shapiro's five levels** (Jan 2026) | L0 manual → L5 "dark factory" for AI-assisted coding. Opinion, not research | [Post](https://www.danshapiro.com/blog/2026/01/the-five-levels-from-spicy-autocomplete-to-the-software-factory/) |
| **Feng, McDonald & Zhang** (2025) | Agent autonomy by user role: Operator → Collaborator → Consultant → Approver → Observer | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12469) |
| **Flatiron Health Agentic Maturity Model** (Nov 2026) | Four layers: Manual, Interactive, Structured, Autonomous, each describing the human's and the agent's role | A practitioner blog post from one company, not research. Summarised in the [companion course](https://pragmaticarun.github.io/ai-native-course/maturity/engineering-team-views/) |

## How to use them well

- **Ladders vs maps.** Ladders (MIT CISR, Gartner, Shapiro) help say where you are. Capability maps (DORA, Google's themes) help decide what to fix next. Use one of each.
- **Mind the caveats.** Vendor and consultancy models can steer toward their offerings; survey-based segments rely on self-reporting; stage names change over time.
- **Different parts of the team will be at different levels**, and that's fine. Aim for the right level per workflow and risk, not a single badge.
- **This course's [self-assessment](/ai-native-teams/start/self-assessment/)** is a conversation starter built from these ideas, not a published standard.
