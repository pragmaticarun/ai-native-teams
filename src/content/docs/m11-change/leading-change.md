## Name the fears honestly

People's concerns about AI are often reasonable:

- **"Will this replace my job?"**
- **"Will I look incompetent if I use it, or if I don't?"**
- **"Will quality drop and I'll be blamed?"**
- **"Am I losing the skills that make me good at my job?"**
- **"Is this ethical / legal / safe?"**

Leaders should address these **directly and specifically**: what the organisation has decided about roles, how time saved will be used, how quality is protected, what support exists. Vague reassurance ("AI won't replace anyone") without commitments tends to destroy trust.

## Make it safe to experiment and to fail

- **Leaders go first**, using AI visibly and sharing their own failures.
- **Celebrate learning, not just wins.** The "one failure" demo in show-and-tell is deliberate.
- **Protect time.** People can't learn new ways of working at 100% utilisation. Reserve time (e.g. a couple of hours a week during the cohort).
- **No surveillance.** Don't use AI-usage telemetry to rank individuals. It drives gaming and fear.

## Align incentives

- If goals reward **output volume**, AI will produce more volume. Reward **outcomes and quality**.
- Recognise people who **build shared assets** (skills, evals, playbook entries), not just individual heroics.
- Make the **time saved** visible and decide together how to reinvest it (quality, learning, customer work, harder problems).

## Working with resistance

Resistance is information. Common patterns and responses:

| Pattern | Often means | Try |
|---|---|---|
| "It's not good enough for our work" | Bad early experience, often weak context or the wrong tool | Pair them with a champion on *their* task; revisit after M3 |
| "It's too risky" | Real risk concerns | Involve them in M9–M10; they often become your best reviewers |
| "I don't have time" | Overload | Protect time; start with a task that saves time immediately |
| Quiet non-use | Fear of looking incompetent | Private, low-stakes practice; peer support |
| Over-enthusiasm without care | Excitement outpacing judgment | Channel into builder role with evals and reviews |

## Communicate in a loop

Share the **why** (the stance), the **what** (the plan and policy), the **how** (training, champions, playbook), and **progress** (metrics, stories), repeatedly. Then **listen**: run short pulse surveys and act visibly on what you hear.
