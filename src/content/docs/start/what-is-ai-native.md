"AI-native" is used loosely. In this course it has a specific meaning.

> **An AI-native team designs its workflows assuming capable AI is available, keeps humans accountable for judgment and outcomes, and measures whether the result is actually better.**

## AI-assisted vs AI-native

| | AI-assisted | AI-native |
|---|---|---|
| Where AI sits | A side tool individuals open sometimes | In the default path of the workflow |
| Unit of change | Individual productivity | The team's process, roles and artefacts |
| Knowledge | In people's heads and scattered docs | Written down so both people and agents can use it (instructions files, skills, specs) |
| Quality | Trust the output, or don't use it | Evals, review policies, and measured outcomes |
| Risk | Ad hoc, or "just don't" | Explicit policy, data classes, permissions, audit |
| Learning | Individuals tinker | Shared playbook, rituals, champions |

## The five shifts

1. **From doing to directing.** People spend less time producing first drafts (code, copy, analyses) and more time specifying, reviewing and deciding. This needs *new skills* (clear specs, critical review), not fewer skills.
2. **From tacit to explicit knowledge.** Agents only know what you put in their context. Teams that write down conventions, decisions and domain knowledge get far better results, and onboard humans faster too.
3. **From outputs to outcomes.** Generating more output is now cheap. The bottleneck moves to review, integration and deciding what's worth doing. Measure outcomes (cycle time, quality, customer impact), not volume.
4. **From trust to verification.** Models are fluent and sometimes wrong. AI-native teams build verification in: tests, evals, citations, human review proportional to risk.
5. **From projects to continuous adaptation.** Models, tools and regulations change every few months. The team needs a rhythm for re-evaluating tools and practices, not a one-off rollout.

## What AI-native is *not*

- **Not "replace people with agents".** Research on AI and software delivery finds that AI amplifies what is already there. DORA's 2025 research summarises this as "AI is an amplifier": strong teams get stronger, and struggling teams see their problems magnified ([DORA 2025 report](https://dora.dev/research/2025/dora-report/)).
- **Not maximal autonomy everywhere.** How much an agent does on its own is a *design choice* per task and risk level (see [M5 · Human-in-the-loop](/ai-native-teams/m05-agents/multi-agent-and-hitl/)).
- **Not buying a tool.** Tools are maybe 20% of the work. The rest is workflow redesign, knowledge, skills, governance and habits.
