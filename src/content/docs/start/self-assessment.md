title: Team self-assessment
description: A 15-minute baseline of where your team is today across eight dimensions.
sidebar: { order: 4 }
---

Do this **before** Module 1, and again at the end of the course. Each person scores alone first (so the loudest voice doesn't set the score), then the team compares and discusses the gaps.

Score each statement **0 (not at all)**, **1 (some people, sometimes)**, **2 (most people, most of the time)** or **3 (it's how we work, and it's written down)**.

## 1. Usage

1. Everyone on the team has access to at least one approved, capable AI assistant.
2. People use AI daily for real work, not just experiments.
3. We know which tasks AI helps with in *our* context, and which it doesn't.

## 2. Skills

4. Most people can write a clear prompt with context, constraints and an output format.
5. People routinely check AI output instead of pasting it straight in.
6. At least two people can build something more than a chat (an automation, an agent, a RAG assistant).

## 3. Knowledge & context

7. Our conventions, decisions and domain knowledge are written down where AI tools can use them.
8. We maintain shared instructions files, prompt libraries or skills for recurring work.

## 4. Workflows

9. At least one end-to-end workflow has been *redesigned* (not just sped up) around AI.
10. Repetitive hand-offs and status work are automated.

## 5. Quality

11. For AI features or automations we run, we have test cases or evals that we re-run when something changes.
12. Review effort is matched to risk (a typo fix and a payment change are not reviewed the same way).

## 6. Safety & governance

13. We have a written AI use policy that people have actually read.
14. People know which data can and can't go into which AI tools.
15. Agents and automations have the least access they need, and risky actions need human approval.

## 7. Operating model & culture

16. There is a named owner (or champions) for AI adoption on the team.
17. We share AI wins *and failures* openly, in a regular ritual.
18. Leadership has communicated a clear stance on AI use (what's encouraged, what's not allowed).

## 8. Value

19. We have a baseline for the outcomes we want AI to improve (cycle time, quality, satisfaction…).
20. We can say, with evidence, whether AI made those outcomes better in the last quarter.

## Reading your score

| Total (0–60) | What it usually means | Focus first |
|---|---|---|
| 0–15 | **Exploring.** Individual, ad hoc use. | M1–M3, M9 (safe usage), M10 (policy) |
| 16–30 | **Adopting.** Regular use, little shared practice. | M3 (shared context), M7 (workflow redesign), M11 |
| 31–45 | **Integrating.** Shared practices, some redesigned workflows. | M5–M6 (agents), M8 (evals), M12 (value) |
| 46–60 | **AI-native.** Redesigned, measured, governed. | Keep the rhythm: re-run M8 and M12 quarterly. |

These bands are this course's own rule of thumb for starting a conversation, not a published standard. [M11](/ai-native-teams/m11-change/maturity-models/) compares the published maturity models.

:::tip[Look at the spread, not just the average]
If one person scores "Usage" a 3 and another scores it a 0, you have uneven access or uneven skills. That gap is often more important than the average.
:::
