## The EU AI Act in one table

The EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) entered into force on 1 August 2024 and regulates AI by **risk level**. It applies to organisations that place AI systems on the EU market or use them in the EU, wherever they're based.

| Tier | Examples | Obligations (summary) |
|---|---|---|
| **Prohibited** | Social scoring, manipulative techniques causing harm, certain biometric uses, emotion recognition at work or school (with exceptions) | Banned |
| **High-risk** | AI used in employment (recruiting, promotion, termination), education, credit scoring, essential services, critical infrastructure, law enforcement; AI as safety components of regulated products | Risk management, data governance, documentation, logging, human oversight, accuracy and robustness, registration; deployers have duties too |
| **Transparency** (Article 50) | Chatbots, AI-generated or manipulated content (deepfakes) | Tell people they're interacting with AI; mark synthetic content |
| **Minimal risk** | Most uses: drafting, coding assistants, summarisation | No specific obligations beyond AI literacy |
| **General-purpose AI models** | The foundation models themselves | Obligations on model *providers* (documentation, copyright policy; more for systemic-risk models) |

**AI literacy (Article 4)** has applied since 2 February 2025: providers and deployers must take measures to support AI literacy among staff who deal with AI systems. A course like this one is part of how teams meet that. The 2026 amendments soften the wording toward *supporting* literacy rather than guaranteeing a level ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)).

## Timeline after the 2026 Digital Omnibus

In 2026 the EU adopted a "Digital Omnibus on AI" that amended the Act's timeline. It reached political agreement in May 2026, received final Council approval on 29 June 2026, and entered into force in late July 2026 ([CSA research note](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-eu-ai-act-omnibus-vii-deadline-delay-20260/), [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/07/27/the-digital-omnibus-on-ai-enters-into-force-today), [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/eu-ai-omnibus-enters-force-amending-ai-act)).

| Date | What applies |
|---|---|
| 1 Aug 2024 | Act enters into force |
| 2 Feb 2025 | Prohibitions; AI literacy |
| 2 Aug 2025 | General-purpose AI model obligations; AI Office operational |
| 2 Aug 2026 | Article 50 transparency obligations (disclosing AI interaction, etc.) |
| 2 Dec 2026 | End of watermarking grace period for systems already on the market; new prohibition on AI that generates non-consensual intimate imagery or child sexual abuse material ("nudifiers") applies |
| 2 Dec 2027 | **High-risk obligations for Annex III systems** (e.g. employment, education, credit). Originally 2 Aug 2026 |
| 2 Aug 2028 | **High-risk obligations for AI in regulated products (Annex I)**. Originally 2 Aug 2027 |

Dates from the sources above as of November 2026. Confirm against the official text on [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj) before relying on them.

## What this means for most teams

- **Most internal productivity use is minimal risk.** Focus on AI literacy, data protection and your own policy.
- **Customer-facing chatbots and generated content** need transparency now.
- **Anything touching hiring, people management, credit, education or access to essential services** is likely high-risk: involve legal early, even with the delay, because building compliant processes takes time.
- **GDPR still applies** to personal data in prompts, training data, logs and outputs.

## Beyond the EU

AI rules are developing in many jurisdictions, including national laws, US state laws, sector rules (finance, health, employment) and guidance from data-protection authorities. Practical approach:

- Ask legal for a **one-page summary of the rules that apply where you operate and sell**, refreshed every six months.
- Track **sector regulators** for your industry.
- Use a framework like NIST AI RMF or ISO/IEC 42001 ([next lesson](/ai-native-teams/m10-governance/frameworks/)) so your practices hold up across jurisdictions.
